# Autonomous Navigation System: Comprehensive Interview Guide

## Top 10 Technical Interview Questions & Answers

### Q1: Why did you choose the Kinematic Bicycle Model over a point-mass or unicycle model?
**Answer:** A point-mass or unicycle model assumes instantaneous holonomic movement or zero-radius turning, which is physically unrealistic for passenger cars and industrial AGVs. The Kinematic Bicycle model captures non-holonomic constraints, wheelbase geometry, and maximum steering limits ($\pm 35^\circ$), ensuring that the paths generated by our planner are kinematically feasible and smooth.

### Q2: What is the difference between Global Planning (A*) and Local Planning (DWA)?
**Answer:**
- **Global Planning (A*):** Runs prior to execution on a static or known map to find an optimal, collision-free route from the origin to destination. It is computationally deliberative and minimizes overall journey distance.
- **Local Planning (DWA / Reactive Potential Fields):** Operates at high frequency (60 Hz) directly on streaming LIDAR sensor readings. It handles dynamic, unpredictable entities (such as crossing pedestrians or sudden obstacles) by nudging the steering angle and regulating speed in real time.

### Q3: How do you handle obstacle inflation, and why is it necessary?
**Answer:** In raw grid planning, path nodes can touch obstacle boundaries. Since our vehicle has finite physical dimensions (width 24 px, length 44 px), a point-sized path through a tight gap would cause the vehicle's edges to collide. We apply an obstacle inflation radius equal to half the vehicle footprint plus a safety clearance margin ($24\text{ px}$), treating the vehicle as a point against expanded obstacles.

### Q4: How does the Pure Pursuit Controller work?
**Answer:** Pure Pursuit calculates a circular arc from the vehicle's rear axle to a lookahead target point on the path. The steering angle is proportional to the sine of the angle $\alpha$ between the vehicle heading and the target vector:
$$\delta = \arctan\left(\frac{2 L \sin(\alpha)}{L_d}\right)$$
Tuning the lookahead distance $L_d$ provides an intuitive trade-off: a smaller $L_d$ tracks aggressive turns closely but risks oscillation, while a larger $L_d$ ensures smooth, stable trajectories.

### Q5: How do you simulate LIDAR in 2D?
**Answer:** We simulate a 360-degree sensor emitting 36 rays spaced at $10^\circ$ increments. For each ray, we calculate analytic ray-slab intersections for rectangular structures and ray-circle intersections for pedestrians and cones. We inject Gaussian noise ($\sigma = 0.8\text{ px}$) to simulate physical sensor imperfections.

### Q6: How does your system calculate Time-To-Collision (TTC)?
**Answer:** For any detected object ahead within the camera's $70^\circ$ Field of View, we calculate the relative closing speed:
$$v_{\text{closing}} = v_{\text{vehicle}} \cdot \cos(\alpha_{\text{rel}})$$
$$\text{TTC} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{v_{\text{closing}}}$$
If $\text{TTC} < 1.8\text{ s}$, the state machine activates collision avoidance deceleration; if $\text{TTC} < 0.8\text{ s}$, the emergency braking system triggers.

### Q7: What are the edge cases of A* search and how are they handled?
**Answer:**
1. **Unreachable goal (blocked by obstacles):** Handled via a direct Euclidean fallback vector with obstacle-stop warning.
2. **Zig-zag paths:** Standard 8-connected grid search generates unnatural jagged steps. We resolved this by implementing line-of-sight path smoothing, reducing waypoint counts by over $60\%$.

### Q8: What is the role of the Longitudinal PID controller?
**Answer:** While Pure Pursuit governs lateral (steering) control, the PID controller regulates velocity. It continuously compares target velocity against current vehicle speed, calculating proportional, integral, and derivative terms with anti-windup clamping to prevent speed overshoot.

### Q9: How can this virtual architecture be ported to physical hardware?
**Answer:** The architecture mirrors ROS 2 (Robot Operating System). The `LidarSensor` maps to the `/scan` topic (`sensor_msgs/LaserScan`), the `CameraDetector` maps to `/camera/image_raw`, the global path maps to `nav_msgs/Path`, and vehicle actuation maps to `geometry_msgs/Twist` (`/cmd_vel`). Replacing the Python simulation interfaces with ROS 2 subscriber/publisher nodes enables direct deployment on a Jetson Nano or Raspberry Pi-powered robot.

### Q10: How do you measure the performance and safety of your navigation pipeline?
**Answer:** We instrument every simulation step with our `MetricsLogger` class. It computes:
1. Trajectory deviation and smoothness.
2. Minimum obstacle clearance distance across all sectors.
3. Near-collision events (safety violations).
4. Trip duration and mean velocity efficiency.
All data is exported into CSV telemetry logs and plotted as multi-panel analytical graphs.
